\nonumchapter{Abstract}

India's digital payment landscape has witnessed a remarkable transformation in recent years, leading to a direct confrontation between traditional physical credit cards and the emerging "Credit on UPI" ecosystem. With the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) allowing the linkage of RuPay credit cards to UPI applications in 2022, the dominance of global card networks like Visa and Mastercard is being actively challenged. This dissertation investigates the shifting consumer preferences and merchant attitudes regarding this transition from physical plastic to digital QR-based credit transactions.

Through a comprehensive analysis of secondary data spanning from 2020 to 2025, coupled with a primary survey involving 250 consumers and 50 merchants in the Delhi NCR region, this study found a significant behavioral shift. The analysis revealed a "bifurcated spending model" among users: consumers heavily rely on the convenience of UPI for daily, low-ticket transactions (averaging around \rupee 1,480), but still prefer swiping traditional credit cards for high-value purchases (averaging \rupee 5,300) to maximize reward benefits.

Furthermore, the data highlighted strong generational differences. Approximately 78\% of Gen Z respondents reported a strong preference for a wallet-free lifestyle, completely depending on their smartphones for credit payments. In contrast, older generations like Gen X showed more resistance, preferring the familiarity of physical cards. However, the study also uncovered critical supply-side friction. Nearly 90\% of surveyed small and medium merchants expressed strong dissatisfaction with the Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) associated with Credit on UPI, viewing it as a severe burden on their already thin profit margins compared to standard, zero-fee UPI payments.

The findings conclude that while traditional plastic cards are losing their absolute monopoly, they are not entirely obsolete yet. The Indian credit market is rapidly evolving into an "invisible credit" system where purchasing power is embedded within smartphones rather than physical cards. The dissertation highlights the urgent need for policymakers to address merchant MDR concerns and for financial institutions to innovate their digital-first credit offerings to sustain this financial evolution.
