\nonumchapter{Abstract}

India's payment system is changing fast, starting what experts call a "form factor war" between regular plastic credit cards and the new "Credit on UPI" system. Since the RBI allowed people to link RuPay credit cards directly to UPI apps in 2022, physical cards from big networks like Visa and Mastercard face a real challenge. This study checks if the ease of scanning a QR code is enough to break our old habit of carrying and swiping plastic cards.

Using RBI data from 2020 to 2025 and a survey of 200 consumers and 50 merchants in Delhi-NCR, the research shows a huge gap in setup: India has only 11 million card swipe machines compared to over 650 million UPI QR codes. The data also shows people use a "bifurcated spending model." They prefer the speed of UPI for daily small purchases under \rupee 2,000, but still use physical credit cards for bigger expenses over \rupee 5,000 to get rewards. Because of this, the average credit card payment is around \rupee 5,300, while UPI is much lower at \rupee 1,480.

A big age difference is speeding up this change. About 78\% of Gen Z regularly leave home without a physical wallet, trusting their phones, compared to just 12\% of Gen X. But there is still a major problem for sellers: 90\% of small merchants dislike the transaction fees (MDR) for accepting Credit-on-UPI, as it eats into their small profits.

Because of these changes, banks and payment apps have to adapt quickly. Instead of pushing physical cards, they are creating virtual credit limits made just for UPI. This shifts how the credit card business works, forcing banks to balance a high number of small UPI payments against the cost of giving rewards, all while dealing with unhappy merchants.

In the end, the study shows physical plastic cards are losing their top spot in India. We are moving toward an "invisible credit" system where your buying power is in your phone, not a piece of plastic. To keep up, banks need to focus on digital-first credit, and policymakers should rethink merchant fees to make things fair for everyone.
