\chapter{Rationale of the Study}

The introduction of \textbf{``Credit on UPI''} directly solves the big gap between who has credit and where it can be used. In June 2022, the RBI officially allowed people to link their RuPay credit cards to UPI apps. This big change means a user can now scan a normal UPI QR code but pay using their credit card limit instead of the money in their bank account. It essentially brings the power of a credit card to every single street corner in India.

\section{Why This Helps Merchants: Zero Setup Cost and More Sales}

For the millions of small shops already taking UPI, this change is a huge deal.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{No Setup Costs:} Shop owners do not need to buy an expensive swipe machine just to accept credit cards. They also do not have to worry about monthly rental fees or paying for a separate internet connection. The cheap QR sticker they already have works perfectly.
    \item \textbf{Bigger Purchases:} People using credit cards usually have more money to spend. This means they might buy more things at once or make impulse purchases, which increases the average bill for the shop.
    \item \textbf{Fee Relief:} For very small purchases under \rupee 2,000, the government often steps in to lower or completely waive the extra transaction fees for the merchant. This makes small shopkeepers much more willing to accept credit without feeling cheated.
\end{itemize}

\section{Why This Helps Banks: Faster Rollout}

For banks, the Credit on UPI model makes it much easier to get new customers and get them to actually use their cards right away.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Instant Digital Cards:} Banks can now issue virtual RuPay credit cards instantly right through a mobile app. They skip the hassle of printing physical plastic, mailing it out, and waiting for activation calls. This is especially appealing to younger customers who want things immediately. In 2024, \textbf{50\%} of all new credit cards were totally virtual.
    \item \textbf{More Frequent Use:} Since scanning a QR code is so easy, people use their credit much more often for small daily things. UPI linked credit cards see an average of \textbf{40 transactions a month}, which is way higher than the 5 times a month people usually swipe a physical card.
    \item \textbf{Better Data:} Every single UPI scan gives the bank helpful information about where and when people shop. This helps them understand spending habits and manage risks better, which could eventually let them offer better personal loans to those exact customers.
\end{itemize}

\section{Why This Helps Consumers: Convenience and Easy Access}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pay Anywhere:} You can finally use credit at street food stalls, auto rickshaws, and tiny shops that never had card machines before. It makes having a credit limit actually useful for everyday life.
    \item \textbf{Simple Experience:} You only need one app and one scan. From there, you just choose whether the money comes from your bank account or your credit line. You also do not have to carry a bulky wallet anymore.
    \item \textbf{Increased Security:} Since there is no physical card to hand over to a cashier, there is zero risk of someone secretly copying your card details or skimming your magnetic stripe.
    \item \textbf{Everyday Rewards:} In the past, you could only earn credit card rewards at big supermarkets or malls. Now, you can earn points on your daily micro purchases like buying milk or paying for a taxi.
\end{itemize}

\section{Academic and Policy Rationale}

This study is happening at the perfect time because:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Policy Check:} The RBI rule from 2022 has been around for about three years now. It is finally old enough for us to see its real impact on the market.
    \item \textbf{Business Strategy:} Banks and payment companies really need solid data right now to decide if they should keep printing physical cards or switch to virtual ones. Printing and shipping plastic is expensive, and this research could help them save millions.
    \item \textbf{Academic Value:} As I mentioned in the research gap, there is not much research on how different digital payment methods compete with each other. This study helps fix that blind spot.
\end{enumerate}
