\chapter{Expected Outcomes}

Based on what I have read and how I planned this research, here is what I actually expect to find at the end of the study.

\section{Expected Findings}

\subsection{1. Validation of the ``Partial Substitution'' Hypothesis}

I expect to find proof that supports my Alternate Hypothesis ($H_1$), but with a slight twist.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Expected Finding:} There will be a strong negative link between UPI growth and debit card usage. This means people are completely dropping their physical debit cards for UPI.
    \item \textbf{Nuance for Credit Cards:} For credit cards, the replacement will only be partial. People will stop using physical credit cards for small purchases under \rupee 2,000, but they will keep swiping them for big purchases over \rupee 5,000.
    \item \textbf{Implication:} This creates a split spending model. UPI takes over the quick convenience economy, while physical plastic cards keep the high value rewards economy.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{2. Confirmation of the Average Spending Gap}

I expect the data to prove that:

\begin{itemize}
    \item The average spending amount on a physical credit card will be much higher, usually around \rupee 5,000 to \rupee 5,500, compared to a normal UPI payment which is usually just \rupee 1,400 to \rupee 1,500.
    \item Credit on UPI will sit right in the middle. The average spend there will likely be around \rupee 2,500 to \rupee 3,500 since it captures those medium sized purchases.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{3. Differences Across Age Groups}

I expect to see these distinct trends based on age:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Gen Z (18 to 25 years old):} A massive preference for UPI, with over 75\% living a completely wallet free lifestyle.
    \item \textbf{Millennials (26 to 40 years old):} A mixed approach where they switch between scanning and swiping just to get the best rewards.
    \item \textbf{Gen X (41 years and older):} Mostly sticking to physical cards out of pure habit and trust.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{4. Merchant Resistance to Fees}

I expect shop owners to react like this:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Over 85\% of small shop owners will strongly oppose paying transaction fees for Credit on UPI.
    \item Over 60\% of merchants will seriously think about returning their physical swipe machines to the bank if more people start scanning credit via UPI.
\end{itemize}

\section{Contribution to Knowledge}

Here is how this project will add value:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Academic:} It will be one of the first deep dives into how digital payments are eating into each other in India. This fills the research gap I mentioned earlier.
    \item \textbf{Managerial:} It will give banks clear advice on whether they should focus on virtual cards instead of plastic and how they should change their reward programs.
    \item \textbf{Policy:} It will give the RBI real data to help them adjust merchant fees so that small businesses do not suffer.
    \item \textbf{Industry:} It will help the companies that make swipe machines predict if their business is actually in danger of dying out. \\ \\
\end{enumerate}

\section{Practical Recommendations (Expected)}

Based on these findings, I expect to suggest the following:

\subsection{For Banks and Card Issuers}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Start a virtual first strategy where physical plastic cards are only printed and mailed if a customer specifically asks for one.
    \item Change reward systems to give points for scanning UPI often, not just for making huge purchases on a physical card.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{For the Reserve Bank of India}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Create a tiered fee structure for Credit on UPI. For example, charge only 0.5\% for purchases under \rupee 2,000, and put a strict 1.5\% limit on bigger purchases.
    \item Run public campaigns to teach everyday consumers that Credit on UPI is actually safe and secure to use.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{For Fintechs and Payment Apps}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Design better app interfaces so users can easily manage their credit card bills right next to their bank accounts.
    \item Clearly show the available credit limit in big text right on the scanning screen so people know exactly how much they can spend.
\end{enumerate}
