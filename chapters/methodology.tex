\chapter{Research Methodology}

I am using a Mixed Method Research Design for this study. Basically, this means I am mixing hard numbers from government data with real opinions from my own primary surveys. This two part way of doing things makes sure that I get a completely full picture of the situation. Looking at just the government numbers tells me *what* is happening, but talking to actual people tells me *why* they are doing it, which is the most important part of this research.

\section{Research Questions}

My study tries to answer these main questions to figure out exactly how the payment landscape is changing:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{RQ1:} How does the growth of UPI transactions compare to physical credit card swipes in India from 2020 to 2025?
    \item \textbf{RQ2:} Is there a real, statistically significant shift where people are actively replacing their physical credit cards with UPI, or are they just using both?
    \item \textbf{RQ3:} What exact things (like pure convenience, better rewards, feeling secure, or checkout speed) make people want to pull out their phone to scan a QR code instead of reaching for their wallet to swipe a plastic card?
    \item \textbf{RQ4:} Are shop owners actually willing to accept Credit on UPI even though they have to pay transaction fees out of their own pockets?
    \item \textbf{RQ5:} Is the average amount spent per transaction really different when comparing physical cards to UPI credit?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Objectives}

\subsection{Primary Objective}

The absolute main goal of this whole project is to see how the introduction of RuPay Credit on UPI is affecting the overall number of physical credit card swipes and altering their future growth.

\subsection{Secondary Objectives}

To help answer that main goal, I also have a few smaller objectives:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Trend Analysis:} To compare how incredibly fast UPI QR codes are spreading across the country compared to the slow rollout of traditional card swipe machines from 2020 to 2025.
    \item \textbf{Consumer Behavior:} To figure out exactly why city shoppers are happily moving away from the old habit of swiping to the new habit of scanning.
    \item \textbf{Merchant Acceptance:} To see if smaller shop owners and street vendors are okay with taking credit payments through QR codes instead of paying rent for bulky POS machines.
    \item \textbf{Demographic Analysis:} To look at how different age groups (like Gen Z, Millennials, and Gen X) prefer to pay, since younger people usually adopt tech faster.
    \item \textbf{Forecasting:} To guess what the future road of physical plastic card printing will be like by looking at how things are trending right now.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Tentative Hypotheses}

Based on my reading and early research, I will be testing these hypotheses using statistical math. Just to clarify, the Null hypothesis is basically saying nothing interesting is happening, while the Alternate hypothesis is what I actually think is going on.

\subsection{Hypothesis 1: The Substitution Effect}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{$H_{0}$ (Null):} There is no real negative link between the number of UPI transactions and the number of physical credit card swipes.
    \item \textbf{$H_{1}$ (Alternate):} There is a significant negative link, meaning as UPI usage goes up, physical card swipes directly go down.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Hypothesis 2: The Ticket Size Differentiation}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{$H_{0}$ (Null):} There is no real difference in the average spending amount between a physical credit card and a UPI linked credit card.
    \item \textbf{$H_{1}$ (Alternate):} People spend significantly more money on average when using a physical credit card compared to a quick UPI linked credit card.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Hypothesis 3: Merchant Preference}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{$H_{0}$ (Null):} Merchants do not really prefer QR codes over physical swipe machines for accepting credit payments.
    \item \textbf{$H_{1}$ (Alternate):} Merchants strongly prefer QR codes because they believe they are much cheaper to set up and maintain over time.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Hypothesis 4: Generational Difference}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{$H_{0}$ (Null):} A person's age has absolutely no effect on whether they prefer UPI or physical cards.
    \item \textbf{$H_{1}$ (Alternate):} Younger people (Gen Z) prefer UPI much more than older people (Gen X) do, mostly because they grew up with smartphones.
\end{itemize}

\section{Research Design}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Type:} Descriptive and Exploratory Mixed Method.
    \item \textbf{Duration of Study:} I am looking specifically at the financial years from 2020 to 2025. This window is perfect because it lets me compare the time before UPI Credit existed to the time after it became popular.
    \item \textbf{Geographical Scope:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Secondary Data:} All of India, using the official numbers published by the RBI and NPCI online.
        \item \textit{Primary Data:} Just the Delhi NCR region. This area is a great testing ground because it has a huge mix of super rich neighborhoods and regular middle class areas, plus a very high rate of digital adoption among local shops.
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\section{Sampling and Techniques}

\subsection{Target Population}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Consumer Population:} People aged 18 to 55 living in Delhi NCR who have an active bank account, own a smartphone, and have made at least one digital payment in the last month.
    \item \textbf{Merchant Population:} Shop owners in Delhi NCR who currently accept some form of digital payment at their checkout counter.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Sampling Technique}

I am using \textbf{Stratified Random Sampling} to make sure my survey reaches all different types of people, rather than just my immediate friends. 

\textbf{Consumer Groups:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Age Groups:} Gen Z (18 to 25), Millennials (26 to 40), Gen X (41 to 55)
    \item \textbf{Income Levels:} Low (under \rupee 5 Lakhs), Middle (\rupee 5 to \rupee 15 Lakhs), High (over \rupee 15 Lakhs)
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Merchant Groups:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Business Type:} Small local street shops versus bigger organized grocery stores.
    \item \textbf{Location:} Busy Central Delhi markets versus the quieter outer NCR suburbs.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Sample Size and Collection Method}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Consumers ($n_1$):} 200 Respondents. I plan to send this out via Google Forms through WhatsApp groups, college networks, and LinkedIn.
    \item \textbf{Merchants ($n_2$):} 50 Respondents. For this group, I will actually walk up to shop owners during their quiet afternoon hours and ask them the questions in person, since they are usually too busy to fill out online forms.
    \item \textbf{Total Sample:} 250 Respondents.
\end{itemize}

I figured out this specific sample size using Cochran's formula. I kept a 95\% confidence level and a 7\% margin of error, which is considered pretty standard and totally acceptable for a college level project.

\subsection{Primary Data Instruments}

Before sending these out to the real sample, I will do a quick test run on five friends just to make sure the wording is not confusing.

\textbf{Instrument 1: Consumer Questionnaire}

I made a simple structured survey using a 5 point Likert Scale (where 1 is Strongly Disagree and 5 is Strongly Agree) to easily measure how people feel.

\textbf{Sample Questions:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item ``I have stopped carrying my physical wallet because I can pay everywhere with my phone.''
    \item ``I would prefer using a Credit Card on UPI over swiping a plastic card if the rewards are exactly the same.''
    \item ``I feel safer scanning a QR code than handing my physical card to a waiter who might walk away with it.''
    \item ``I find it annoying to dig out my physical card just for a tiny small purchase.''
    \item ``I actually know that my RuPay credit card can be directly linked to my UPI app.''
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Instrument 2: Merchant Interview Schedule}

This is a brief conversational interview with both scaled questions and open questions where they can complain or explain their thoughts.

\textbf{Sample Questions:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item ``The monthly rent for a card swipe machine is a big financial burden on my business.'' (Likert)
    \item ``I prefer customers to pay via UPI because the money hits my bank account faster.'' (Likert)
    \item ``If Credit on UPI charges you a 2\% fee, would you still let customers use it at your shop?'' (Yes, No, or Maybe)
    \item ``What exactly would make you finally return your physical card machine to the bank?'' (Open ended)
\end{enumerate}

\section{Variables}

In statistics, independent variables are the causes or the given facts, and dependent variables are the effects or the outcomes we are trying to measure.

\subsection{Independent Variables}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Total UPI Transaction Volume
    \item Total QR Code Count across India
    \item Age Group of the Consumer taking the survey
    \item Income Level of the Consumer
    \item Type of Merchant Store (Unorganized versus Organized)
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Dependent Variables}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Physical Credit Card Transaction Volume
    \item Growth Rate of Card Swipe Machines
    \item How much consumers actually prefer UPI over cards (The Preference Score)
    \item How willing merchants are to accept Credit on UPI
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Moderating Variables}

These are extra factors that might change how the independent and dependent variables interact with each other.

\begin{itemize}
    \item How secure the user actually feels using their phone
    \item If good credit card rewards are being offered
    \item If the merchant actually knows about the hidden transaction fees
\end{itemize}

\section{Organization of Data Analysis}

I have organized the data analysis work into two main phases to keep things clear and manageable:

\subsection{Phase 1: Macroeconomic Analysis (Secondary Data)}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Building the Data:} I will collect the huge spreadsheets of monthly numbers from the RBI and NPCI websites from April 2020 all the way to March 2025.
    \item \textbf{Making Charts:} I will create colorful line graphs and bar charts in Excel to easily visually show who is growing faster.
    \item \textbf{Math Calculations:} Figuring out the five year compound annual growth rates for UPI, Credit Cards, swipe machines, and QR codes.
    \item \textbf{Testing Connections:} Using Pearson's $r$ in SPSS to check if my very first substitution hypothesis is actually correct.
    \item \textbf{Spending Analysis:} Calculating the average ticket size by taking the total money spent and dividing it by the exact number of transactions.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Phase 2: Behavioral Analysis (Primary Data)}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Cleaning up:} Reading through the data and deleting incomplete survey responses or crazy answers that do not make sense (like if someone just clicked 'Strongly Agree' for every single question without reading).
    \item \textbf{Who took the survey:} Making simple pie charts to show the ages, genders, and incomes of my respondents.
    \item \textbf{Checking Reliability:} Using Cronbach's Alpha math to make sure my survey questions actually measure what they are supposed to measure and make sense together.
    \item \textbf{Running Tests:} Doing the heavy t tests, Chi square math, and ANOVA calculations on the final cleaned answers.
    \item \textbf{Comparing Groups:} Looking very closely at the results to see if rich Gen Z kids answer totally differently than middle class Gen X adults.
\end{enumerate}
