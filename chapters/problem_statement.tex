\chapter{Statement of the Problem}

The financial system in India is currently going through a big battle over how people pay. The old system, which relies on physical card swipe machines (POS terminals), is expensive to set up and mostly found only in big cities. On the other hand, the new system powered by QR codes is incredibly cheap and can be found absolutely everywhere.

\section{The Infrastructure Asymmetry}

This study looks at a core issue I call \textbf{``Infrastructure Asymmetry.''} This simply means there is a growing gap between how many credit cards banks can give out and how many shops can actually accept them.

\subsection{The Physical Ceiling: Limitations of POS Terminal Deployment}

The old credit card system relies completely on Point-of-Sale (POS) swipe machines. These machines cost a lot of money to set up and keep running.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{POS Statistics:} As of late 2024 and 2025, India only has about \textbf{8.9 million to 11 million} deployed POS machines.
    \item \textbf{Growth Rate:} The number of these machines is growing, but very slowly. They only increase by about 17\% to 23\% each year.
    \item \textbf{Cost Structure:} Buying a new machine costs a lot upfront, usually between \rupee 8,000 to \rupee 12,000. Shop owners also have to pay a monthly rent of \rupee 300 to \rupee 500, plus internet fees to keep it connected.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The Digital Explosion: The Proliferation of QR Codes}

In total contrast, the UPI system has grown massively. This is because it is built on simple Quick Response (QR) codes that do not cost much to make.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{QR Code Statistics:} The number of UPI QR codes put up across India has jumped to somewhere between \textbf{633 million and 657 million}.
    \item \textbf{Growth Rate:} QR codes are being adopted incredibly fast. They are growing by \textbf{91.5\% to 126\%} every single year.
    \item \textbf{Cost Structure:} A QR code is basically free. It is just a printed paper sticker that costs absolutely nothing to maintain.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The Asymmetry Gap: 11 Million vs. 650 Million}

This huge difference creates a big problem for accepting payments. Right now, there are about \textbf{60 times more QR codes than swipe machines} in India.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The ``Credit Class'' Exclusion:} Before Credit on UPI started, a person with a regular credit card could only shop at the 11 million places that actually had a swipe machine.
    \item \textbf{The ``Debit Class'' Ubiquity:} Meanwhile, someone just using normal UPI from their bank account could pay at over 650 million different spots.
\end{itemize}

\section{The Core Problem}

Big banks and networks like Visa and Mastercard have spent billions of dollars over many decades building their plastic card systems. If people stop using physical cards, all those expensive machines and networks will become useless. Also, the whole credit card business relies on making money from merchant transaction fees (MDR). If UPI makes paying with credit too easy and cheap, the big credit card companies will face a serious threat to their profits.
