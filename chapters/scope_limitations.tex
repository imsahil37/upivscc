\chapter{Scope and Limitations}

\section{Scope of the Study}

\subsection{Geographical Scope}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Secondary Data:} Looking at the whole country of India using official government data from the RBI and NPCI.
    \item \textbf{Primary Data:} My actual survey is only focused on the Delhi NCR area, which includes Delhi, Gurgaon, Noida, and Faridabad.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Temporal Scope}

\begin{itemize}
    \item The study looks at the five financial years from 2020 to 2025.
    \item This specific timeline lets me see what things were like before Credit on UPI existed and compare it to how things changed after it became popular.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Subject Matter Scope}

\begin{itemize}
    \item The study focuses strictly on physical, in person shopping at retail stores.
    \item \textbf{What I am skipping:} Business to business payments, online bank transfers, ATM cash withdrawals, and online shopping where you just type in your card numbers.
    \item \textbf{What I am including:} Actually swiping a physical card at a machine, scanning a normal UPI QR code, and scanning a QR code to pay with a linked credit card.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Demographic Scope}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Consumer respondents:} Regular people between the ages of 18 and 55.
    \item \textbf{Merchant respondents:} Shop owners running both small local stalls and bigger organized stores.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations of the Study}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The Data is Very New:} Paying with credit on UPI only really started getting popular in late 2023. Because it is so new, there is no long term data out there yet. I am basically looking at early trends, which might shift as more people get used to the system.

    \item \textbf{People Might Exaggerate:} Sometimes people taking surveys want to look smart or highly tech savvy. Because of this, they might claim they use digital payments more than they actually do. This is a pretty common problem in survey research.

    \item \textbf{Big City Bias:} Since I only surveyed people in the Delhi NCR area, my results might look a bit skewed. People in big cities usually have better internet and newer phones than people in rural towns, so the adoption rates I found are probably higher than the national average.

    \item \textbf{Only RuPay Cards Work:} Right now, the government only allows RuPay credit cards to link with UPI apps. Visa and Mastercard have way more users, but they are not allowed yet. This limits the full picture of what would happen if everyone could link their cards.

    \item \textbf{Government Rules Might Change:} The government currently helps pay the transaction fees for tiny purchases to keep merchants happy. If they suddenly stop paying those subsidies, merchants might stop accepting these payments entirely, which makes it hard to predict the future.

    \item \textbf{Small Sample Size:} I only surveyed 250 people in total (200 shoppers and 50 shop owners). This is totally fine for a college level project, but it is definitely too small to perfectly represent the entire massive population of India.

    \item \textbf{No Online Shopping:} I completely ignored e-commerce. Buying things on Amazon is a totally different game where physical cards compete against saved card details, not QR codes, so I decided not to tackle that here.

    \item \textbf{Things Change Too Fast:} The tech world in India moves incredibly fast. By the time this study is done, new things like Visa on UPI or offline digital payments might become huge and completely change the rules of the game.

    \item \textbf{Merchants Might Hide the Truth:} Shop owners might not admit if they secretly charge customers extra fees for using a card, mostly because they are scared of getting in trouble with the regulators.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Delimitations}

Just to keep this project doable and focused, I purposefully decided to leave a few things out:

\begin{itemize}
    \item I am not trying to explain the deep technical computer coding behind how UPI or banking networks actually work. I only care about how normal people use them.
    \item I mention other countries like Brazil and China just to give some background context, but I did not actually collect any primary data from foreign markets.
    \item I am not looking at whether individual banks or payment apps are actually making a financial profit from all of this.
\end{itemize}
